This page is pretending to be a website

Click the purple icon in the bar above — that is exactly what the Overlay Ink toolbar button does in Brave. The transparent layer appears on top of this page, with a small toolbar of its own: click anywhere and the caret lands right there, so notes go where you put them.

Try this

  1. Click anywhere on this page — a caret appears exactly there and you type in place. Click somewhere else and that becomes a second note; they never run into each other.
  2. Pick an ink colour, size or font from the top toolbar. With a note selected it changes that note; with nothing selected it sets the style for the next one.
  3. Drag a note's ⠯ grip to move it anywhere, or ✕ to delete it.
  4. Hit Esc or click View — every note stays put and this page becomes clickable again.
  5. Click the icon again (or press Alt+Shift+H) to hide it all. Your notes are remembered.

What it is

  • A fully transparent layer — only your writing is drawn, nothing else.
  • A canvas, not a single text box: write in the margin, beside a paragraph, wherever it makes sense, in whatever colour you like.
  • Notes follow you to every site and every tab, and stay on your machine — they live in the extension's local storage.

Why the page stops responding in Edit mode

While you place and type notes, clicks have to reach the overlay instead of the links underneath, otherwise the page would navigate away mid-sentence. That is what Edit mode does. Switch to View and every click goes back to the page, so you can keep reading, scrolling and clicking with your notes floating on top of everything.

The toolbar at the top of the overlay stays clickable in both modes, and it fades out of the way in View mode until you hover over it.

Built for reading

Long passages of text are here on purpose: colour, size and the text outline option exist because a note needs to stay readable over a white article, a dark terminal or a photo. Set the background slider above 0% if you want a faint panel behind the words — every note keeps its own colour, so one canvas can hold a red reminder and a blue question.

This demo runs the extension's real content script with a small stand-in for the browser APIs, so what you see in the overlay is the shipped behaviour. In a real installation the toolbar icon in Brave toggles it on any page (browser pages like brave://settings are off limits, as always for extensions).
